Our mission

Engineering should not be something you discover in college.

Most students meet engineering for the first time in a college intro course, long after they have already decided what they are and are not good at. By then, the question is no longer “is this interesting?” but “am I already behind?”

NextGen Engineering exists to move that first encounter about eight years earlier — and to make it a good one. Not a lecture about engineering, but an afternoon spent actually doing it.

Our story

Started by students, for students.

NextGen Engineering was founded by high school students who had spent years in robotics, CAD, and embedded electronics, and kept running into the same thing: younger students were curious and capable, but had nowhere to start.

So we started teaching. What began as a handful of sessions became a curriculum, and the curriculum became this library. We are still students ourselves, which turns out to be the point — we remember exactly which parts were confusing.

What makes us different

Every session ends with something built.

A lot of STEM outreach is a slideshow and a demo. Ours is not. Students leave having modeled a part, wired a circuit, or made a motor turn under their own instructions.

  • Always free. No fees for students, schools, or families — ever.
  • We bring everything. Boards, components, tools, and laptops if you need them.
  • Open curriculum. Every lesson is published here for anyone to use or adapt.
  • Real tools. Students use the same CAD and hardware that working engineers use.
  • Small groups. We cap sessions so every student gets hands on the hardware.
